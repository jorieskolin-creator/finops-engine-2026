FinOps Assessment Engine · Current Architecture

Evidence first. Scores second. Strategy last.

A governed enterprise-grade assessment engine with deterministic, evidence-gated reasoning, designed to produce trusted and actionable decisions in complex enterprise environments. Unlike traditional AI agents that rely on autonomous generation, the FinOps Engine operates through a controlled reasoning pipeline: source material is parsed into a source registry and A-F evidence packets, evidence is extracted, findings are independently verified, scores are calculated through deterministic logic, and outputs pass through strict quality gates. Every maturity claim must trace back to real source evidence; Knowledge Base material can guide interpretation and planning without becoming proof of current state.

Privacy-safe local parsing first
Files are parsed before analysis: PDF text through PDF.js, CSV/TSV/XLSX through the local Tabular Evidence Profiler, HTML/JSON as text, and images as source visuals. PDFs are checked for parse quality so scanned or sparse-text pages are visible as source-coverage warnings. Large tables are profiled locally across all rows and only compact aggregates plus targeted evidence rows are forwarded to models. The Engine builds a source registry with stable source, page, sheet, row, and chunk IDs, then runs deterministic full-source DLP scanning plus distributed high-risk model review samples. Low-relevance material is allowed to run, but it should produce insufficient evidence rather than a forced rejection.

Phase 01Forensic Audit
6 parallel audits · Claude Sonnet 4.6
Six FinOps domains are scanned in parallel. Each batch receives its own A-F context packet containing likely relevant chunks, source references, parse-quality notes, table profile evidence, and absence-test coverage hints. Each batch evaluates 5 maturity criteria and 5 anti-pattern criteria. The auditor extracts candidate evidence from uploaded material only; the Knowledge Base is not allowed to become source evidence. Uploaded material is treated as untrusted content: it can be quoted as evidence, but it cannot instruct the engine.

Phase 1.5Evidence Check
GPT-5.5 evidence-check profile · independent verifier
The scanner does not grade itself
Each batch is checked against cited source chunks before metrics are calculated. Evidence is classified as supported, weak, unsupported, or missing. If citation location is unclear, the verifier can fall back to the full source registry. If a score is over-supported, the engine runs one targeted rescan for the affected criteria. If support still fails, the criterion is downgraded deterministically before Phase 2. This is why a scanner can be optimistic without the final score becoming optimistic.
Anti-pattern absence is handled carefully
  • Finding means harmful behavior was found.
  • Partial finding means weak or partial harmful signal exists.
  • Tested absent means relevant evidence exists and the anti-pattern was meaningfully tested but not found.
  • Not assessed means absence is unknown, not automatically good.

Phase 02Evidence-Gated Scoring
Pure deterministic · no AI
Mathematics, not model opinion
Phase 2 converts only verified audit logs into metrics. Readiness is capped when evidence density is low, so weak or irrelevant material cannot accidentally produce a healthy score. Low anti-pattern burden helps only when absence has actually been tested.
Outputs
  • Evidence-Gated Readiness and maturity depth
  • Anti-Pattern Burden, clearance, coverage, and burden confidence
  • Evidence Density as verified source coverage, including quote-backed gaps and tested anti-pattern absences
  • Delivery Integrity, silent areas, confirmed gaps, and category footprint
  • Classification: Insufficient Evidence, Crawl, Walk, or Run
  • Quality signal: enough evidence to synthesize, or findings mode only
Validated metrics become locked facts
Phase 03Synthesis & Roadmap
Sonnet summaries · Opus roadmap
Strategy is bracketed by confidence
Evidence summary and diagnosis are written separately from roadmap planning. Summary uses source evidence and Phase 1/2 metrics only. Roadmap synthesis is a dedicated stage: Claude Opus 4.7 first, GPT-5.5 fallback, Claude Sonnet 4.6 second fallback. The roadmap explains WHY, WHAT, and HOW, and HOW actions must trace to locked findings. Tactic IDs are used only when an exact Knowledge Base match is supported; no tactic ID is better than a wrong tactic ID. The remote PDF Knowledge Base, tactics database, and tactic activity playbook can enrich planning, but they cannot prove customer current state.
Confidence behavior
  • HIGH: evidence summary, diagnosis, planning decision, scorecard, and roadmap.
  • MEDIUM: cautious planning with assumptions and validation needs.
  • LOW: findings mode only, NO_GO, missing-evidence guidance, and empty roadmap.
Candidate report must survive validation
Phase 3.5Fact-Check, Sanitation & Quality Gate
GPT-5.5 primary · deterministic aggregation
GPT-5.5 checks summary and roadmap claims independently. If a medium-reasoning fact-check would otherwise create a hard strategy BLOCK, the Engine can retry with a high-reasoning GPT-5.5 fact-check profile before final aggregation. Unsupported roadmap actions, mismatched tactic IDs, and unsupported WHY/WHAT wording are removed, rewritten, or quarantined in the appendix before the final gate. The Quality Gate returns GO, WARN, or BLOCK. WARN keeps usable score and roadmap content visible; BLOCK means the visible report is unsafe or evidence is too weak. Fact-check and quality-gate details are retained for traceability, but the readable report keeps them in the appendix.
Reliability layer
  • Model routing is centralized and each stage has fallbacks.
  • Internal model calls can recover completed results after response-stream closure.
  • Session recovery, safe report import, and editable privacy review reduce the risk of losing or leaking a completed assessment.
  • Detailed evidence-check, fact-check, tactic-grounding, and strategy-hygiene notes move to the Quality & Strategy Hygiene Appendix.
