Section 01

The Idea: FinOps Engine Inside the FinOps Assessment

The Evidence-Driven FinOps Assessment helps an organization build an honest, evidence-backed view of how it manages cloud costs today, spot the highest-leverage improvements, and agree on a practical path forward.

The FinOps Engine is the governed analysis layer supporting that assessment—not a standalone self-service grading tool and not a general-purpose chatbot. Its job is to acquire, screen, verify, and structure approved customer evidence so FinOps practitioners spend less time digging through files and more time validating findings and guiding decisions with the customer.

The Core Principle AI stages propose bounded candidate findings, while deterministic code owns admission, scoring, gates, and audit lineage. The Engine never replaces FinOps expert judgment, and the customer always keeps final decision authority.

How the Engine Fits Into the 3-Day Assessment

Step 0

Prepare & Align

Agree on scope and goals, walk through how data handling and privacy controls work, and gather existing documents.

Day 1

Understand & Collect

Run stakeholder workshops and interviews to understand real day-to-day practices and identify supporting evidence.

Day 2

Analyze & Clarify

Run the FinOps Engine on the collected material, close accidental evidence gaps, and calibrate findings with FinOps experts.

Day 3

Validate & Commit

Review the Summary and Master Data reports with the customer, prioritize actions, and agree on a FinOps Change Plan.

Six Cloud-Neutral Domains (30 Capability & Anti-Pattern Pairs)

The Engine evaluates evidence across AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, SaaS, and multi-cloud environments using six domains (60 criteria total: 30 Maturity capabilities paired with 30 Anti-Pattern indicators):

A
Cost Visibility & Allocation Tagging, cost allocation, reporting, showback/chargeback, and unit economics (A1–A5 / AP-A1–AP-A5).
B
Rate & Usage Optimization Commitments (RIs/Savings Plans), rightsizing, waste reduction, and storage efficiency (B1–B5 / AP-B1–AP-B5).
C
Governance & Policy Budgeting, forecasting, policies, FinOps operating model, and procurement (C1–C5 / AP-C1–AP-C5).
D
Architecture & Engineering Cost-aware design, automation, IaC guardrails, and scalable engineering practices (D1–D5 / AP-D1–AP-D5).
E
Culture & Organization Roles, accountability, cross-team collaboration, executive sponsorship, and enablement (E1–E5 / AP-E1–AP-E5).
F
GenAI & AI Cost Management Token/inference spend, model routing, AI budgets, guardrails, and unit value (F1–F5 / AP-F1–AP-F5).

Note on Domain F when AI is not in use: If an organization has no GenAI workloads or uploads no AI evidence, Domain F skips model dispatch locally and stays Not assessed—it is never averaged in as zero maturity, though its 10 criteria (5 pairs) remain part of the 60-criterion coverage and 30-pair Resolution denominators.

How Customer Data, Identifiers & Financial Records Are Handled Original source files, full spreadsheets, and image pixels are parsed locally in your browser (raw images are never sent to AI models, and filenames are replaced with pseudonymous Document NNN IDs). Before any governed model packet is assembled, deterministic privacy and DLP controls block prohibited secrets (private keys, cloud keys, API tokens, credentials) and redact pattern-matched identifiers (emails, phone numbers, IP addresses, government/bank IDs, contextual person names, billing/invoice account IDs, and explicit contract-rate phrases such as negotiated discount rates, contract values, and EDP pricing).

What enters governed AI packets: Only privacy-screened text, bounded model-eligible table rows (kept with row locators so findings and quotes can be independently verified), and approved deterministic table summaries are sent in SHA-256-bound text packets to the configured LLM providers. Canonical packet bodies in PostgreSQL and recovery checkpoints in Redis are deleted on acknowledged report delivery, explicit session reset, or expiry, while content-free run metadata and hashes remain for 90-day audit retention. Always screen inputs and use the built-in Privacy Review panel before sharing reports externally.

How Different File Types Are Handled (Practical Tips)

  • Show real practice, not just rules: A policy shows what is intended; a billing or tagging table shows current coverage; an exception review or completed action log proves the control operates in practice. Combining them produces corroborated findings.
  • Spreadsheets & Delimited Tables (XLSX, CSV, TSV): The Engine preserves sheet and physical row numbers, inspects structure locally, and runs authoritative deterministic analyzers where live criterion bindings exist (such as A1 / AP-A1 row-weighted and cost-weighted tagging/allocation coverage and bound tabular checks) alongside privacy-screened row evidence. Workbook formulas use cached values, and hidden sheets, hidden rows/columns, or unfiltered non-evidence structures are privacy-scanned locally and withheld from model packets.
  • Screenshots & Scanned PDFs (Local OCR): Browser-local Tesseract OCR extracts English and Finnish text from PNG, JPEG, WebP, and sparse/scanned PDF pages (up to 30 OCR pages per PDF). Because raw images are never sent to AI models, chart lines, colors, and non-text visual shapes remain explicitly withheld as uninspected visual regions.
  • Skip unrelated "noise" files: Avoid uploading unrelated purchasing logs, generic industry articles, or FinOps textbooks. The Engine enforces customer FinOps scope, so generic reference material adds clutter without earning credit.
  • Use interview notes as evidence leads: Workshop notes help identify day-to-day friction and point you toward the real exports, tickets, or policies to upload as corroborating proof.
Section 03

Why You Should Never "Invent" Information or Documents

If a policy, report, or operating practice does not exist yet, do not write a quick placeholder document just to feed the Engine.

Golden Rule: "Silence is Data" The FinOps Engine evaluates what an organization actually has and operates today—not what someone could draft on the morning of the assessment. Missing documentation is an informative diagnostic signal, not an error.

What Happens When You Stay Honest

  • Unassessed criteria remain NA / Not assessed—never artificially penalized as zero and never assumed healthy.
  • Domains with no qualifying customer evidence skip model dispatch locally and are clearly listed for evidence collection.
  • Stakeholders can hold a productive Day 3 conversation: Is this practice informal, undocumented in another tool, or genuinely absent?
  • The resulting roadmap or validation plan targets real operational priorities.

Why Invented Docs Backfire

  • Aspiration does not prove operation: Drafts, proposals, and unapproved plans cannot substantiate operational maturity criteria.
  • Triggers pair contradictions: Newly written claims conflict with real billing tables and operational logs, surfacing explicit pair contradictions.
  • Distorts the roadmap: Overstating current controls suppresses foundational tactics the team actually needs first.
Section 04

How to Read & Understand the Gauges

Under ADR-002, the Engine uses a resolution-based maturity model across 30 registered capability/anti-pattern pairs. Rather than collapsing unknowns into zeroes or capping scores behind roadmap gates, it keeps pair maturity, assessment resolution, anti-pattern friction, and roadmap actionability distinct.

1. The Headline Gauges & Supporting Views

Gauge / Metric What It Computes How to Read It
Corroborated Maturity Weighted maturity across only fully resolved pairs—pairs where both the capability criterion and its paired anti-pattern criterion have provenance-bound resolution (including governed tested absence). Shows maturity where both capability execution and anti-pattern control are proven together. Returns N/A if no pair has both sides resolved yet.
Observed Maturity
(with Resolution)		 Weighted maturity across fully or partially resolved pairs (1.0 weight credit when both sides are resolved, 0.5 when one side is resolved), paired with Overall Assessment Resolution %. Always read Observed Maturity alongside its Resolution %. A high score at low resolution means "strong in a narrow evidenced slice, unknown across the rest of the estate." Unknown criteria remain NA.
Adjusted FinOps Maturity & Stage
(Crawl · Walk · Walk with significant friction · Run)		 Observed Maturity × √(Resolution)—discounts Observed Maturity by how much of the 30-pair framework is resolved, and drives stage classification only when Assessment Sufficiency passes. Publishes Crawl, Walk, Walk with significant friction, or Run only when both Criterion Evidence Density (60 criteria) and Overall Assessment Resolution (30 pairs) strictly exceed 24% with complete provenance and verification; otherwise shows Insufficient evidence.
Anti-Pattern Burden, Coverage & Clearance Separates confirmed operational friction from missing evidence across the 30 anti-pattern indicators. Confirmed / Partial Present: Friction evidenced in the customer records.
Tested Absent: Scope was inspected and proven clear of the anti-pattern.
Not Assessed: Uncovered in the files. Low burden at low coverage means "untested," not "problem-free."
Acquisition Quality Telemetry
(Master Data HTML)		 Observability snapshot of the input package itself, kept separate from customer maturity scores. Reports Extraction completeness, Evidence coverage (X/60), Acquisition quality composite (verified strength + source diversity + category diversity), Provenance integrity, and KB readiness.
Domain Balance (A–F) & Evidence Categories Compares maturity vs. anti-pattern signals per domain and tracks the seven evidence categories (Policy, Process, Operational, Automation, Accountability, Financial-Integration, Cultural). Quickly highlights imbalances—for example, heavy Policy documentation with little Operational or Automation evidence.

2. Two Separate Decisions: Assessment Sufficiency vs. Quality Gate

Assessment Sufficiency (PASS | BLOCK) decides whether evidence is broad enough to publish a Crawl / Walk / Run classification. Quality Gate (GO | WARN | BLOCK) is a separate decision governing roadmap actionability—it never caps or rewrites maturity scores.

GO

Actionable Roadmap

Strong verified coverage (36+ of 60 criteria assessed, i.e. 60%+ density) with verified fact-checking and all required Playbook tactics resolved. Ready for expert calibration and customer planning.

WARN

Use With Caution

Moderate coverage (15–35 of 60 criteria assessed), partial anti-pattern coverage, packet coverage warnings, or claim-sanitation notes. Produces a cautious roadmap with explicit assumptions to validate first.

BLOCK

Findings Mode / Do Not Execute Yet

Triggered when evidence is below the 25% floor (0–14 of 60 criteria assessed—switching output to Findings Mode with scoped observations and an evidence-collection plan instead of a directive roadmap) or when fact-check/required-tactic blockers remain unresolved.

Why a BLOCK Result Is Still Constructive A BLOCK result protects teams from acting on guesswork. It pinpoints exactly which records were omitted or which controls lack operating evidence so the team knows what to verify next.
Section 05

How Key Summaries Are Created (Behind the Scenes)

Instead of feeding raw files into a single prompt, the FinOps Engine runs a multi-stage governed pipeline where models author bounded semantic judgements and deterministic code enforces contracts, math, and gates.

1

Local Parsing, Table Inspection & Privacy Gate

Your browser fingerprints each file, extracts text, tables, and EN/FI local OCR, runs bound deterministic table analyzers, redacts pattern-matched identifiers, and blocks prohibited secrets before any model call.

2

Bounded Retrieval & Domain Audit (A–F)

Up to two pre-audit retrieval rounds assemble A–F packets (using catalog-only gap queries when needed). Domains with eligible evidence audit 60 criteria (180 sub-questions); empty domains record Not assessed locally.

3

Independent Evidence Check & Re-Scan

An independent verifier checks candidate findings and quotes against registered customer sources and approved derived calculations, triggering targeted re-scans or conservative downgrades when support is weak.

4

Deterministic ADR-002 Scoring

Code reconciles provenance and computes Corroborated Maturity, Observed Maturity with Resolution, Adjusted Maturity, Assessment Sufficiency, and anti-pattern metrics. Models cannot alter calculated scores.

5

Summary, Personas & Playbook Roadmap

Role-routed models (Reasoner, Workhorse, Quality Checker) author the Evidence Summary, Diagnosis, Persona Lenses, and—when permitted—a roadmap grounded in the 23-tactic Playbook (or Findings Mode when blocked).

6

Fact-Check, Required Tactics & Quality Gate

Final verification scrubs unsupported claims, enforces required Playbook tactic dispositions (with one bounded roadmap repair if needed), builds the RunTrace lineage record, and sets GO / WARN / BLOCK.

Strict Separation of Truth

Layer 1

Customer Evidence

Privacy-screened customer records and approved substantive table calculations are the only source of truth capable of resolving customer scores or quotes.

Layer 2

FinOps Knowledge Base

Defines evaluation rubrics and what good FinOps looks like. Reference KB material is strictly prohibited from being cited as customer proof.

Layer 3

Tactic Playbook (23 Tactics)

Governed remediation actions (TAC IDs) activated after scoring is locked. Confirmed anti-patterns can require tactics (ADR-003); tactics never alter maturity scores.

  • Full Report View & Downloads: Export Summary Report HTML, Master Data HTML, Assessment JSON, and RunTrace JSON.

How to Utilize the Five Report Views in Practice

1. Evidence Summary (Facts First)

What It Contains Locked maturity and resolution gauges, Assessment Sufficiency status, key metrics, confirmed strengths, scoped observations, confirmed gaps, and unassessed/silent areas.
How to Use It Open Day 3 here so stakeholders align on verified facts first, and check silent areas to spot any existing customer records that were simply omitted from the upload.

2. FinOps Diagnosis (The "Why")

What It Contains Cross-domain synthesis of the primary bottleneck, underlying root causes, domain-level explanations, and diagnostic confidence.
How to Use It Move discussion beyond isolated symptoms (such as untagged resources) to structural causes (such as missing ownership handoffs between Finance and Engineering).

3. Persona Lenses (FinOps Lead · CFO · Engineering Lead)

What It Contains The same verified evidence framed for operational cadence (FinOps Lead), financial risk and governance without invented savings promises (CFO), and engineering trade-offs (Engineering Lead).
How to Use It Share each lens with the matching stakeholder group so Finance, Engineering, and FinOps work from one consistent factual baseline.

4. Optimization Roadmap — or Findings Mode (The "What Next")

What It Contains When Quality Gate permits: phased steps structured into WHY (evidence link), WHAT (target outcome), and HOW (Playbook actions and assumptions).
When blocked by thin evidence (Findings Mode): evidence-backed observations plus a concrete validation and evidence-collection plan.
How to Use It Use as a structured starting point in Day 3—prioritize by business value, prerequisites, and team capacity, then assign owners and dates in the customer's FinOps Change Plan.

5. Master Data Report, Acquisition Telemetry & RunTrace

What It Contains Criterion-by-criterion audit findings, source quotes with page/sheet/row locators, acquisition quality telemetry, tactic dispositions, and content-free stage/hash lineage.
How to Use It Reference during Day 2 calibration or whenever a stakeholder asks "Which file and row proved that score?" or "Why was this criterion marked NA?"
Saving & Reopening Assessments: Display-Only HTML vs. Restore JSON Downloaded Summary HTML and Master Data HTML files are strictly display-only (no embedded hidden JSON or scripts, making them clean to share). To reopen a saved assessment in the FinOps Engine later, download the Assessment JSON file as well—selecting that .json file via Add Files on the home screen restores the full interactive session.
Section 07

Quick-Start Checklist

  • Gather real operating artifacts (2–30 files): Stay within 25 MB per file / 50 MB total, up to 100 PDF pages (max 30 scanned/OCR pages), mixing policies, budgets, optimization logs, architecture reviews, and allocation tables.
  • Remove credentials before upload: Deterministic privacy blocks keys/tokens and redacts pattern-matched identifiers and negotiated-rate phrases, while routed text and table rows are sent in governed packets for quote verification—never upload live secrets.
  • Never invent missing documents: Leave undocumented areas out so the Engine honestly records NA / Not assessed rather than distorting scores or roadmap priorities.
  • Unlock & choose routing: Unlock with the assessment password in the header and enable Deep analysis when you want deeper roadmap synthesis upfront.
  • Read Observed Maturity, Resolution %, Sufficiency, and Quality Gate together: Check how much of the 30-pair framework resolved and whether the roadmap gate is GO, WARN, or BLOCK.
  • Run Privacy Review & download both HTML and JSON: Scrub organization/person names if needed, export display-only Summary & Master Data HTML for stakeholders, and keep the JSON export to reopen the session later.
